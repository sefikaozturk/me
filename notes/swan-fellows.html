notes from swan fellows

November 23, 2026

These are some of my notes from the Swan Fellows program, which I am currently at! I will add more as time goes on.

Robotics

The first day was mostly socializing, and then we had a session with Jay from Robocurve, which I found to be the most interesting session as robotics was my entire life for about two years.

Jay talked a lot about robotics evals that focus on safety aspects. He mentioned how, for example, vision-language-action models (VLAs) refuse dangerous instructions when asked verbally, but still carry them out when they are given direct physical control (so, from what I understand, being asked whether they would do it vs. actually being given access to a robotic arm). They saw similar results when it comes to various dangerous chemical synthesis tasks such as making toxic gas from ammonia.

This made me start thinking more about “physical jailbreaks” - in this paper they are described as instructions whose words seem harmless but are actually unsafe once deployed in the physical world.

A survey says guardrails designed for text can't be assumed safe for a body, and I could totally see this being a much bigger problem within just one year, especially if I accept Jay’s argument that we’ll have robots capable of doing everything a 15 year old does within the next year at most.

LLMs are already very capable of controlling robots and succeed greatly at VLA benchmarks - we’re basically only bottlenecked on hardware, which, you could argue, could be largely solved by more advanced intelligence as well.

Though, China already has a large humanoid fleet and control of the supply chain for actuators and the rare earth magnets inside them. Jay believes that for the US, even with superior models by 2027, there isn't enough time to build a competing supply chain. Another concern that was mentioned in the discussion was that if advanced models’ weights sit on the hardware of a humanoid model that is e.g. deployed in military and sent to a war, an adversary could capture a humanoid and reverse-engineer the model, similar to the ASML machine leak. Interesting!

Starting an org

This program is focused on helping people start companies that benefit AI safety. There’s apparently a lot of stuff to think about when thinking about building an org that can scale that I hadn’t considered as being “important” variables previously. I’ve always had an anti-bureaucratic stance to things (life, ideas, projects, people, anything (?)).

For example, I thought that even having a name isn’t really that important until you need to fully incorporate. But founders can often end up needing to incorporate quite early in order to legally accept certain types of funding and run certain operations, or at least this happened to me, and maybe it was just a lack of planning.

Some questions that were brought up in the discussions were: What does success mean for us? What would make us say, over coffee with someone, "it's going really well"? An org needs to define fields of success and what they’ll measure those against. One obvious reason for this is that these metrics are what they’ll present when fundraising etc. and it’s good to be able to fully convey why your work is good (hopefully it is good!)

One heuristic that a participant shared was that a new person who joins the org should be able to pick up from wherever you are at in a project and follow the rest based on various process flows you created for all processes you have. They suggested that having flowcharts is actually good, which is so contrary to how I tend to think about starting orgs (move fast and break things)! However, it is starting to look quite reasonable.

Media! The media coverage of your org is highly dependent on you. Many orgs have full time employees just working on shaping media coverage. It doesn’t seem optimal to me that as an org you have so much space to influence the media coverage about you.

An org is also expected to have its own view on things! An employee of your org may be asked for your opinion on the U.S. bombing Iran during a live interview, so you ideally need a company stance that is cohesive that people can refer to if need be.

CRM! Attio is a good option if you need more database-shaped automations rather than email inbound/outbound automations. It is also free for up to 3 team members, which is good if you’re just starting out and trying things. It is very important to keep a good record of everyone / every org you talked to about your product/service, what you talked about, date of your initial contact, where in your sales pipeline they are, etc.

For-profit?

A big topic throughout the discussions in the program is whether to start a for-profit or a non-profit org. Arguments for starting a for-profit org is that you have tighter feedback loops with reality and the markets are efficient. Arguments for starting a non-profit is that you are less bound to the demands of the stakeholders and the ever-changing markets, and there’s a big flow of capital into philanthropy. IMO you should pick whichever structure allows you to reach your goals as fast as possible.
